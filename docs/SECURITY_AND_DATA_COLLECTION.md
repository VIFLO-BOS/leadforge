# LeadForge Security and Data Collection Policy

Status: binding; sections 2, 4, 6 and 7 updated for what shipped in Phase 2, sections 2, 3, 6, 7, 10 and 12 for Phase 3 (discovery, credits, Google Places terms check), sections 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10 and 12 for Phase 4 (SafeFetch, website analysis, enrichment, scoring, duplicate review), and sections 2, 3, 4, 7, 10 and 12 for Phase 5 (CRM, bulk operations, async exports, usage, reports), section 8 for Phase 6 (billing), and sections 1, 3, 9, 10, 11, 12 and the new section 13 (the Phase 7 review and its findings) for Phase 7, and sections 2, 3 and 13 for Stage 8 (lead list visibility, new filters, download redirects), and sections 1, 2, 8 and 13 for Stage 9 (real retry time on 429s, sign-in redirect for signed-out download links, resumable subscription checkout), and sections 2, 7 and 13 for Stage 10 (organization data export, deletion workflow and purge). Last updated 2026-10-28.

This document is binding on implementation. Where it conflicts with a feature request, the feature changes.

## 1. Authentication

- Passwords hashed with Argon2id (memory 64 MiB, 3 iterations, parallelism 1). Minimum 10 characters; common passwords, keyboard sequences, a single repeated character and passwords containing the email's local part or the user's name are refused. (The Phase 0 plan named zxcvbn score 3; release 1 uses these rules instead, section 13 finding 2.)
- Access JWT (EdDSA, 15 minutes) plus rotating refresh token (30 days, hashed at rest, session family tracked). Reuse of a rotated refresh token revokes the whole family.
- Cookies: httpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax; refresh cookie path-restricted.
- Email verification required before any organization data is accessible. Password reset invalidates all sessions.
- Revocation is immediate (Phase 7): signing out, signing out a device, a password reset and refresh-token reuse detection list the revoked sessions in Redis for the access-token lifetime, and the access tokens of those sessions are refused at once. Routine refresh rotation is not a revocation.
- Login throttling per account and per IP; generic error messages; constant-time comparisons. Per IP: 10 sign-in attempts per minute, counted on the address Express derives from the socket and exactly `TRUST_PROXY_HOPS` proxy hops (never the client-supplied left part of `X-Forwarded-For`). Per account: 10 failed attempts per 15 minutes from any addresses, then 429. Unknown emails still run one Argon2 verification so timing does not reveal accounts. Stage 9: the 429 carries the real wait (`Retry-After` and `details.retryAfterSeconds`, the time left on the lock; the route limits send the time left on the throttler's block). The lock is keyed on a hash of the email whether or not an account exists and is checked before the account is looked up, so a locked unknown email gets the same status, body shape, message and timing as a locked account; the wait reveals only when the first counted failure for that email happened, which the caller made. The failure counter is written with its expiry atomically (a counter found without one is given a full window), so a lock can never become permanent.
- Cross-site requests (Phase 7): state-changing requests a browser marks as cross-site (`Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site`, or an `Origin` other than `APP_URL` or `API_URL` from browsers without Fetch Metadata) are refused with 403 `CSRF_FAILED` before routing. This covers the public routes the session-bound CSRF token cannot (sign-in, sign-up, reset), so another site cannot sign a victim into an attacker's account. Server-to-server webhooks are exempt.
- Production requires `COOKIE_SECURE=true` (the environment schema refuses to start otherwise).
- No OAuth providers in release 1. A reserved `users.auth_providers` jsonb column allows adding them without a breaking migration.

Implementation:

- `@RequirePermission("businesses:write")` decorator plus `PermissionsGuard`. A lint rule fails CI on any route handler without a permission decorator or an explicit `@Public()`.
- Organization resolution from `X-Organization-Id` is verified against `memberships` on every request; cached 60 seconds per user and org pair with invalidation on membership change.
- Repositories take `organizationId` as a required positional parameter. An ESLint rule bans importing the raw Mongo `Db` or `Collection` and `PrismaClient` outside `packages/db-*` repositories.
- Background job payloads include `organizationId`; processors re-check organization status, and membership of the requesting user when the job acts on their behalf. Discovery (Phase 3): payloads are validated with Zod and the job is loaded with the payload's `organizationId`; a malformed payload or a job id that is not in that organization fails without retries before any work. Before a round starts and before every page, the worker re-checks that the organization is live and that the job's creator is still an active member holding `discovery:run`; otherwise the job fails with `ACCESS_REVOKED` and its credits are settled.
- Phase 3 permission keys: `discovery:run` (owner, admin, researcher) for estimate, create, cancel and retry-failed; `businesses:read` for job lists, detail, items, the all-jobs list, `GET /sources`, `GET /discovery/sources` and the credit balance; `settings:sources` (owner, admin) for enabling and disabling sources; `usage:read` (owner, admin, researcher, analyst) for the credit ledger; `notifications:read` (every role) for the user's own notifications, which are additionally filtered by user.
- Phase 4 permission keys: `analysis:run` (owner, admin, researcher) for requesting website analyses (single and bulk), website-contacts runs, email checks and the per-business refresh; `dedup:write` (owner, admin, researcher) for merge, reject, defer, undo and scans; `settings:scoring` (owner, admin) for editing the scoring profile and requesting a recompute; `businesses:read` (every role) for analyses, runs, scores, the scoring profile, the duplicate queue and merge history. Undo is further limited in the service: owners and admins can undo any merge, others only a merge they performed, within 30 days (otherwise 403). The worker re-checks before every analysis and contacts run that the organization is live and the requester is still a member holding `analysis:run`; otherwise the run fails with `ACCESS_REVOKED` and an analysis credit is released. Queue payloads are validated with Zod and loaded with the payload's `organizationId`; a malformed payload or an id outside that organization fails without retries.
- Stage 4: `businesses:write` (owner, admin, researcher) for bulk suppression (`POST /businesses/suppress`, at most 100 ids; ids outside the organization come back `not_found`, never touched); `businesses:read` for the wizard catalogues `GET /discovery/categories` and `GET /discovery/locations`. `GET /public/data-licenses` and its texts are public (licence texts must be available to anyone receiving the data). The Overture staging catalogue and its sync runs are platform-level reference data with no `organizationId`; no route writes them, discovery reads them, and anything an organization keeps is copied into its own org-scoped records (docs/DATABASE_DESIGN.md section 1).
- Queues: every BullMQ queue and worker uses the `QUEUE_PREFIX` key prefix (default `leadforge`), so separate stacks on one Redis never process each other's jobs.
- Exports are generated server-side from filters re-evaluated under the requester's permissions (in the API when the export is requested and again in the worker when it is built); download links are signed, single-organization, single-user and valid 15 minutes. Sales Representatives (`exports:create_assigned`) export only businesses whose lead is assigned to them, through both the synchronous CSV and async export jobs.
- Phase 5 permission keys and rules:
  - `settings:pipeline` (owner, admin): `PUT /pipeline/config`.
  - `pipeline:write` (owner, admin, sales rep): stage moves, lead edits, activities and outreach. `leads:assign` (owner, admin, researcher): assignment. Creating a lead needs either; researchers cannot choose a stage, and a sales rep's leads are assigned to the rep.
  - Members without `leads:assign` (sales reps) work only leads assigned to them: moves, edits, activities and outreach on another lead are `403`, and their bulk stage moves skip other leads (`not_assigned_to_you`). Read access (lists, pipeline, activities, outreach, reports) stays with every role, as the matrix says.
  - `lists:manage` (owner, admin, researcher) manages every list; `lists:manage_own` (sales rep) manages the lists the rep owns ("own and assigned": created by them, or handed to them by a manager through `ownerUserId`) and cannot hand a list to someone else. Analysts manage no lists.
  - Assignees must be active members whose role can work leads (owner, admin, sales rep), verified in PostgreSQL on every assignment (`422 INVALID_ASSIGNEE`).
  - Bulk operations: the route needs one of `lists:manage`, `lists:manage_own`, `leads:assign`, `pipeline:write`; the service then requires the action's own permission. The worker re-checks the requester's active membership and permission (and list ownership) before running; otherwise the operation fails with `ACCESS_REVOKED`.
  - Exports: `exports:create` (owner, admin, researcher, analyst) or `exports:create_assigned` (sales rep). Owners and admins see every export; others see only their own (others' ids are `404`). The download route is reachable only signed in; it verifies the HMAC-SHA256 signature over (organization, export, user, expiry) in constant time before anything else, then the expiry, then current membership and export permission in PostgreSQL, then visibility. The signing key is `EXPORT_SIGNING_SECRET`, or derived from `CSRF_SECRET` with a fixed label.
  - Activities can be edited or deleted by their author or an owner or admin (`members:manage`); system stage changes and assignments cannot.
  - Usage (`usage:read`: owner, admin, researcher, analyst); reports and overview (`businesses:read`).
  - Where the matrix was silent the conservative choice was made and documented here: researchers cannot log activities (matrix: activities are owner, admin, sales rep), analysts cannot create lists, and lead creation for a suppressed business is refused.
- Phase 6 permission keys and rules:
  - `billing:manage` (owner, admin): billing overview, plans, checkout, payments and their verification, invoices and their signed downloads, subscription cancel, resume and the card page. `billing:refund` (owner only): refunds. The matrix's "Billing, checkout, cancel subscription" row is owner and admin; refunds were not in the matrix, and the conservative choice (owner only) was made.
  - `GET /billing/banners` needs only `businesses:read`; roles without `billing:manage` see only the negative-balance banner (it pauses their discovery and analysis work) without a link.
  - Entitlements are checked in the service on every relevant request: member cap on invitations, acceptance (under a row lock on the organization) and member reactivation; discovery and website analysis switches; async export row cap. The browser only displays them.
- Stage 8 (2026-10-27), lead list visibility, checked and written out (the behaviour did not change):
  - Reading lists is open to every role for every list of the organization, as the matrix's first row says ("View businesses, lists, pipeline, reports"): list pages, list details, list items, and the `listId` filters of the pipeline board, leads, activities and outreach. The matrix is explicit here, so the restrictive default for silent cases does not apply. Reading a list exposes nothing beyond directory data every role already reads.
  - The two sales representative limits in the matrix are enforced on the server: "Manage lead lists: own and assigned" (changes, including bulk list actions, need `lists:manage` or ownership; otherwise `403`) and "Export: assigned only" (any list, filter or selection is cut to the rep's assigned leads, `scope: "assigned"`). Bulk stage moves skip leads that are not the rep's.
  - The web app's "my lists" view filters by `ownerUserId` for usability; it is not the access boundary. Tested in `phase5-access.test.ts` ("Lead list visibility for sales representatives") for all five roles.
- Stage 8 signed download links opened by a browser: a failure with a download code answers `303 See Other` to a fixed page of the web app, `<APP_URL>/app/jobs/exports/<id>?downloadError=<code>` or `<APP_URL>/app/billing?downloadError=<code>`. The location uses only `APP_URL`, the validated id and a code from a fixed list, never the request's host, forwarding headers, referrer or query, so it cannot be an open redirect. It is sent only for navigations (`Sec-Fetch-Mode: navigate` or an HTML-preferring `Accept`); every other failure and every other request keeps the JSON error.
- Stage 9 signed-out navigations to those links (`401 UNAUTHENTICATED` or `TOKEN_EXPIRED`) answer `303 See Other` to `<APP_URL>/sign-in?next=/app/jobs/exports/<id>` or `<APP_URL>/sign-in?next=/app/billing`, under the same rules: `APP_URL`, a fixed path and the validated (lowercased) export id only, never the request's host, forwarding headers (`X-Forwarded-Host`, `-Proto`, `-Prefix`), referrer, origin or query (a `next` or `redirect` parameter in the link is ignored), so it cannot be an open redirect (tested with spoofed headers in `exports-async.test.ts` and `billing-flows.test.ts`). The authentication guard refuses before the export or invoice is read, so the redirect is identical for ids that do not exist. A malformed export id and every request that is not a navigation keep the JSON `401`. The web app's sign-in page accepts `next` only as a same-origin path.
- Stage 10 permission keys and rules:
  - `organization:delete` (owner): request (typed name and current password) and cancel the organization deletion. `organization:export` (owner): request, poll and download the organization data export; the signed download re-checks it. Admins have neither.
  - `@PausedDuringDeletionGrace()` routes answer `409 ORGANIZATION_PENDING_DELETION` while a deletion is pending, after the permission check: discovery create and retry, website analysis, enrichment and refresh, CSV upload and commit, checkouts, subscription resume, invitations (and accepting one, checked in the service). The list is in docs/API_CONTRACTS.md, "Organization deletion and data export".
  - Once the purge starts the organization is closed (`deleted_at`): `OrgScopeGuard` refuses it (`403 ORG_FORBIDDEN`) and workers stop at their next membership check.
- The frontend hides controls by permission for usability only. No authorization decision is made in the browser.

## 3. Tenant isolation testing

A dedicated suite seeds two organizations and asserts, for every list, detail and mutation endpoint, every export and every job type:

- Org A cannot read, update, delete or enumerate Org B resources by ID. Cross-org IDs return 404, never 403, to avoid revealing existence.
- Filters cannot be crafted to leak counts (a `listId` from another org yields 404, not an empty result).
- Phase 3 coverage: discovery job detail, items, cancel and retry-failed, and notification mark-read return 404 across organizations (and across users for notifications); job, all-jobs, ledger, balance and notification lists only show the caller's organization; a discovery queue payload naming another organization's job is rejected and writes nothing (`apps/api/test/discovery-access.test.ts`).
- Phase 4 coverage (`apps/api/test/phase4-access.test.ts`): another organization's businesses, analyses, enrichment runs, duplicate candidates and merges return 404 on every Phase 4 route (detail, latest analysis, score, runs, refresh, merge, reject, defer, undo); analysis, candidate, merge and job lists show only the caller's organization; filters naming another organization's business are 404; a bulk analysis naming another organization's business skips it as `NOT_FOUND` and holds nothing; scoring profiles are per organization. The same file checks the permission matrix on every Phase 4 route for all five roles.
- Phase 5 coverage (`apps/api/test/phase5-access.test.ts`): the permission matrix on every new route for all five roles; another organization's leads, lists, list items, activities, outreach records, bulk operations and exports return 404 on every route (detail, edit, delete, move, assign, items, attempts, download link); every list endpoint shows only the caller's organization; filters naming another organization's list, business or lead are 404; bulk selections naming another organization's businesses report `not_found` and touch nothing; export selections of them match 0 rows; reports count only the caller's organization; a download link used by another user, or with its organization swapped, is `DOWNLOAD_LINK_INVALID`; bulk and export queue payloads naming another organization's operation or export fail without retries and write nothing.
- Stage 8 coverage: `GET /pipeline/board?listId=` naming another organization's list is `404` (`phase5-access.test.ts`, `route-isolation.test.ts`). The directory's `ids` filter naming another organization's businesses matches nothing (`200`, empty, total 0), exactly like unknown ids, so nothing is revealed (`businesses.test.ts`, `phase5-access.test.ts`, `route-isolation.test.ts`). Bulk result rows never carry another organization's business name (`bulk.test.ts`). Another organization's member opening a victim's signed export or invoice link gets `303` to their own app with only the id they sent and `DOWNLOAD_LINK_INVALID` (`route-isolation.test.ts`).
- Job payloads with a mismatched `organizationId` are rejected before any work.
- Phase 7 completion (`apps/api/test/route-isolation.test.ts`): the route list comes from Nest's controller metadata and a table classifies every route (public, user-scoped or organization-scoped, its declared permission, and the cross-organization probes for it); a route added without an entry, or whose permission changes, fails the suite. For every organization route, another organization's scope header is 403 `ORG_FORBIDDEN`; for every route that takes an id (81 probes), another organization's id is 404 or a documented no-op that returns nothing of the other organization; every list, aggregate, report and settings read (41) shows none of the other organization's ids, names, members or marker text; every non-public route refuses anonymous callers; every state-changing route outside the 8 exemptions refuses a request without the CSRF token; signed export and invoice links and invitation tokens stay bound to their organization and user; and the victim organization's MongoDB documents and PostgreSQL rows are byte-for-byte unchanged at the end. The same suite classifies every queue job type declared in `QueueJobMap` (24) and runs each organization-scoped handler (12) with the attacker's organization and the victim's resource: each refuses or finds nothing, and the attacker's data gains no reference to the victim.

## 4. Input handling

- All request bodies, queries and params validated with Zod before reaching services. Unknown keys are stripped.
- Mongo queries are built from allowlisted fields; user text never becomes an operator; regex input is escaped and anchored.
- CSV import: size cap 20 MB, 50,000 data rows per file in release 1 (per-plan caps arrive with entitlements in Phase 6), 100 columns, cell length cap 2,000, 30 uploads per hour per user. A row whose mapped cell starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, tab or carriage return is rejected with `FORMULA_INJECTION` and reported (phone and WhatsApp columns may start with `+`). Every row is validated in full; invalid rows are reported per row and never imported partially or dropped silently. The importing user must attest a lawful basis on commit.
- Export neutralizes formula injection by prefixing `'` to every cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, tab or carriage return, except a strict E.164 number (`+` followed only by 7 to 15 digits), which cannot carry a formula. Exports are capped at 1,000 rows synchronously and 10 per hour per user (synchronous and async each), re-evaluate filters under the caller's organization, and never include suppressed businesses. Async exports are capped per organization by the `export_row_cap` entitlement (default 10,000); they are built as a ZIP whose CSV gets the same neutralization, and stored with keys that are validated plain relative paths (no traversal) in object storage that is never publicly readable. Bulk operations touch at most 5,000 businesses, resolved and frozen when the operation is created.
- HTML from collected pages is parsed with `parse5` or `cheerio` only. It is never rendered or executed. Evidence snippets are stored as text and escaped on display.
- File uploads: CSV only in release 1 (`.csv`, `.tsv`, `.txt`), checked by extension, declared MIME type and content sniffing (Excel workbooks, PDFs, images, archives and binary data are refused). The file itself is not stored: rows are parsed in memory and staged in MongoDB (`import_rows`, deleted 30 days after upload); only the file name, size and SHA-256 are kept on the import record. Uploads are parsed after authentication, CSRF, organization and permission checks.

## 5. Website analysis and SSRF prevention

Shipped in Phase 4. Every fetch of a third-party page (website analysis, website-contacts enrichment, robots.txt) goes through `SafeFetch` (`packages/core/src/safe-fetch`), which runs only in the worker. The API never fetches third-party pages.

1. Parse the URL with the WHATWG parser; allow only `http:` and `https:`; reject userinfo (`user:pass@`); allow ports 80, 443, 8080 and 8443 only. Encoded IPv4 forms (`http://2130706433/`, `0x7f.0.0.1`) are normalized by the parser and then checked like any literal.
2. Refuse host names before any DNS lookup: `localhost`, `metadata.google.internal`, `metadata`, `instance-data`, `kubernetes.default`, anything under `.localhost`, `.local`, `.internal`, `.lan`, `.home.arpa`, `.intranet`, `.corp`, and single-label names. Resolve A and AAAA through c-ares (`dns.Resolver`, not the OS resolver; 5-second timeout); a name that resolves to nothing fails with `DNS_FAILED`. Reject the host when **any** resolved address falls in `0.0.0.0/8`, `10.0.0.0/8`, `100.64.0.0/10`, `127.0.0.0/8`, `169.254.0.0/16`, `172.16.0.0/12`, `192.0.0.0/24`, `192.0.2.0/24`, `192.88.99.0/24`, `192.168.0.0/16`, `198.18.0.0/15`, `198.51.100.0/24`, `203.0.113.0/24`, `224.0.0.0/4`, `240.0.0.0/4`, `255.255.255.255`, `::/128`, `::1/128`, `64:ff9b::/96`, `64:ff9b:1::/48`, `100::/64`, `2001::/32` (Teredo), `2001:db8::/32`, `2002::/16` (6to4), `fc00::/7`, `fe80::/10`, `fec0::/10`, `ff00::/8`. IPv6 forms that embed an IPv4 address (IPv4-mapped `::ffff:a.b.c.d` in dotted or hex form, IPv4-translated `::ffff:0:a.b.c.d`, IPv4-compatible `::a.b.c.d`, NAT64, 6to4, Teredo) are unwrapped and the IPv4 address is checked too; mapped, translated and compatible forms are refused even for a public IPv4. IP-literal URLs get the same checks. Code: `ip-policy.ts`.
3. Connect to the address that was checked: the request uses a fixed `lookup` that returns the pinned address (IPv4 first), keeps the original `Host` header, and sets TLS SNI and certificate verification to the host name. Every connection (page, robots.txt, each redirect hop) resolves and checks again; a record that flips to a private address between connections is refused (DNS rebinding test in `safe-fetch.test.ts`).
4. Follow at most 5 redirects manually (301, 302, 303, 307, 308), re-running steps 1 to 3 and the robots check on every hop.
5. One 10-second deadline covers connect and read (`SAFE_FETCH_TIMEOUT_MS`). Bodies are streamed and aborted past 2 MiB, counted after decompression, so a compression bomb is stopped; a declared `Content-Length` over the cap is refused before reading. Only `text/html`, `application/xhtml+xml`, `text/plain` and `application/json` are read (robots.txt is parsed whatever its declared type, cut at 500 KiB per RFC 9309). No connection pooling (`agent: false`, `Connection: close`).
6. robots.txt per RFC 9309: groups are matched on the product token of the user agent (`LeadForgeBot`), falling back to `*`; the longest matching rule wins and `allow` wins a tie; `*` and `$` are supported. A 4xx robots.txt means no rules; a 5xx or a redirect loop means disallow all (re-checked after 1 hour); when the host cannot be reached at all the fetch reports that failure and nothing is cached. Policies are cached per origin in Redis for 24 hours (`lf:robots:<origin>`). A disallowed page is never requested; an analysis whose home page is disallowed fails with `ROBOTS_DISALLOWED`, records `robots.allowed = false`, and its credit is released.
7. User agent `SAFE_FETCH_USER_AGENT`, default `LeadForgeBot/1.0 (+<APP_URL>/legal/data-collection)`; the env schema requires it to start with a product token. Rate limits through Redis token buckets shared by every worker: 1 request per 2 seconds per host (`SAFE_FETCH_HOST_RPS`, `www.` ignored) and 10 per second overall (`SAFE_FETCH_GLOBAL_RPS`), applied to every request including robots.txt and redirects. A request that would wait more than 60 seconds fails with `RATE_LIMITED` and the job is retried. At most 5 pages per analysis (home, then the first same-site contact, about, services or menu, and booking links) and 3 per contacts run (home, contact, about).
8. No cookies are stored or sent, no `Authorization` header, no client certificate. `Accept-Encoding: gzip, deflate, br`.
9. No JavaScript execution and no headless browser in release 1. HTML is parsed with `parse5` into a bounded view (20,000 elements, 256 levels deep); it is never rendered or executed.
10. Page content is data. No LLM consumes it. Pages live only in worker memory during the job and are never stored. What is kept: for analyses, the detections with evidence snippets of at most 300 characters (a CSS selector path, a URL, a short text or header excerpt), the URLs and statuses of the pages visited, redirects, TLS issuer and expiry; for contacts runs, the extracted values as `website` observations with `{ page, selector, snippet }` evidence (snippets at most 200 characters). Analyses and observations follow the business record's retention.
11. Development and tests only: `SAFE_FETCH_HOST_OVERRIDES` (`host=ip:port`) serves exact host names from a local address so a local test site can be analyzed; it bypasses DNS and the deny list for those names only, and the env schema refuses it when `NODE_ENV=production` (the production SafeFetch also ignores it).
12. The worker runs with a network-level egress allowlist where the host supports it: docs/DEPLOYMENT.md section 7 (allow 80 and 443 to the public internet; deny private ranges, link-local metadata addresses and internal services).

Contact verification rules applied to what SafeFetch collects (PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS section 5): a phone becomes `verified` only when it is valid for its numbering plan and was found on the business's own website (the home page's final host is the business's website domain or a subdomain); WhatsApp becomes `confirmed` only from a `wa.me`, `api.whatsapp.com` or `whatsapp:` link naming the number, or a number written right after an explicit "WhatsApp" label, on the own site; a team member's assertion stays `user_asserted`. Emails are checked for syntax, MX (a null MX counts as none) and a disposable domain (`disposable-email-domains-js`, CC0-1.0, a packaging of github.com/disposable-email-domains/disposable-email-domains); each check is a verification event and the email stays `unverified`, because release 1 does no mailbox (SMTP) probing. Discovery status is never used as proof of verification.

## 6. Data collection policy

Principles:

- Collect only business-facing information: business name, category, address, business phone, business email, website, social profile URLs, opening hours, description.
- Do not collect personal names, personal phone numbers, personal emails or employee data from sources. Where a sole trader's business contact is also personal, it is handled as business contact information under legitimate interest, and suppression requests are honored.

## 7. Privacy, rights and suppression

- Suppression list per organization (domain, phone, email, business) checked at ingest (matching import rows are skipped and reported), on corrections, on website contacts (a newly found identifier that is suppressed suppresses the business), on merge (the survivor is re-checked, and a suppressed side always leaves the survivor suppressed) and at export. Suppressed businesses are never fetched: analysis and contacts requests skip them (`SUPPRESSED`), and a business suppressed while queued fails its run and releases the credit. Lifting a suppression removes only the entries created from that business.
- CRM and suppression (Phase 5): contact activities (call, WhatsApp, email, meeting) and outreach records for a suppressed business, or to a phone number or address on the organization's or the platform's suppression list, are refused with `409 CONTACT_SUPPRESSED`. Choice: block, with an explicit, audited override. A member may still record the contact by sending `suppressionOverride.reason` (10 to 500 characters, for example "they called us"); the reason is stored on the activity and outreach record and written to `audit_events` as `contact.suppression_overridden`. Notes are always allowed. Suppressed businesses cannot be added to the pipeline, added to lists, or assigned in bulk, and are never exported. Recording an outreach status of `opted_out` does not suppress the business; suppression stays an explicit action.
- Platform-wide do-not-collect list, maintained by operators, for businesses that request removal; applied across all organizations.
- Public removal and correction request form on the data collection legal page, stored as `contact_submissions` with `kind = data_request`; 30-day handling target; operator tooling under `/admin`.
- Data minimization: observation values for retention-limited sources are redacted when the retention window ends; metadata is kept. Shipped in Phase 3 as the `purge-expired-content` job (maintenance queue, every 10 minutes), which also removes expired values from business records; reads and exports hide them as soon as they expire.
- Organization data export (JSON and CSV) and an organization deletion workflow (14-day grace, then purge Mongo by `organizationId` and anonymize PG users while billing records are kept for the statutory period). Shipped in Stage 10:
  - **Export** (owner only, `organization:export`, because the file holds every member's email): one ZIP per request built in the worker, one at a time per organization, kept 7 days, downloaded through a 15-minute signed link bound to the owner, the organization and the export, and re-checked at download (still an owner). Values whose source retention has ended are left out exactly as reads hide them; CSV cells are formula-safe; `ATTRIBUTION.txt` and `LICENSES/` travel with the open data; payments carry no card fields and the audit log no IP addresses or user agents.
  - **Deletion request** (owner only): the organization's exact name, typed, and the owner's current password (re-authentication; wrong passwords count toward the per-account sign-in lock and answer `403 REAUTH_FAILED`, never 401). Owners and admins are told in-app and by email at once and about 3 days before the purge; any owner can cancel until the purge starts. During the 14-day grace, new discovery, analysis and enrichment, imports, checkouts and plan changes, and invitations are refused (`409 ORGANIZATION_PENDING_DELETION`); reads, exports and CRM work continue.
  - **Purge** (worker, resumable steps recorded in `organization_erasures`): the organization is closed first (no member can open it), queued work is removed and running work stopped, Paystack renewal is disabled, export files are deleted, every MongoDB document with the organization's id is deleted from every organization-scoped collection derived from the schema registry (the audit log excepted), memberships, invitations, preferences, API keys and usage rows are deleted, and members who belong to no other organization are anonymized (email replaced by a unique `.invalid` placeholder, name and password cleared, sessions revoked). Kept: payments, invoices (with their frozen bill-to) and invoice PDFs, refunds, disputes, the credit ledger, `billing_audit`, the organization's audit events, and the organization row itself, renamed. Members of other organizations keep their accounts. An erasure receipt without personal data is written to object storage (outside the database backups) so operators can re-apply the deletion after a restore (docs/OPERATIONS.md). Details: docs/DATABASE_DESIGN.md, "Organization deletion (Stage 10)".
- Access to exports and to contact fields on detail views is written to `audit_events`.

## 8. Billing security

Shipped in Phase 6 (`packages/core/src/billing`). PostgreSQL holds every piece of billing state; MongoDB holds only notifications and application audit events about it (ids and statuses, never amounts or balances as state).

- **Keys.** The Paystack secret key (`PAYSTACK_SECRET_KEY`, `sk_test_...` or `sk_live_...`, validated at boot) exists only on the server and is never logged, returned or put in an error message (provider failures become `502 PAYMENT_PROVIDER_ERROR` with a generic message). The redirect flow means no public key is needed. Without a key, billing reports "not configured", checkout and webhooks answer `422 BILLING_NOT_CONFIGURED`, and nothing is faked. `PAYSTACK_BASE_URL` can point at a local fixture server only outside production (the environment schema refuses another origin in production).
- **Webhook authentication.** The app is created with Nest's `rawBody` option, so the JSON parser keeps the exact request bytes on `req.rawBody` (it applies to every JSON request; only the webhook route reads it). The signature is HMAC-SHA512 of those bytes with the secret key, hex, compared with `x-paystack-signature` in constant time (`timingSafeEqual`, after a length and hex-format check). Unsigned, malformed or wrongly signed requests get `401 WEBHOOK_SIGNATURE_INVALID` and are not stored, so unauthenticated traffic cannot fill the event table. Optional `PAYSTACK_WEBHOOK_IP_ALLOWLIST` (compared with `req.ip`, which honours exactly one trusted proxy hop, not a client-supplied `X-Forwarded-For`) answers `403` to other addresses; the signature is required regardless. The route is public, CSRF-exempt (it is not a browser request) and rate limited like every route.
- **Never grant on a redirect or a payload.** The return page only queues a verify. Every grant follows Paystack's transaction verify by reference returning `success` with the same reference, amount (integer kobo) and currency as our payment row. A mismatch grants nothing, flags the payment, writes `billing_audit` and an audit event, alerts owners and admins and logs an error; reconciliation never settles a flagged payment.
- **Idempotency at four layers:** webhook event uniqueness (provider plus derived event key), `payments.provider_reference` and `payments.idempotency_key` uniqueness (checkout `Idempotency-Key` per organization and user; renewals `renewal:<reference>`), the payment status transition under the customer row lock, and `credit_ledger.idempotency_key` uniqueness (`payment:<id>:grant`, `refund:<id>:reversal`). Tested: the same `charge.success` delivered 10 times concurrently gives one event row, one payment, one grant; 10 concurrent settlements grant once.
- **Server-side prices.** Prices, plan codes and credit amounts come from the database (`plans`, `credit_packs`, frozen on the payment row at checkout); the client sends only `planId` or `packId`, and unknown body fields are rejected. Placeholder or zero prices are never charged on a live key (`PRICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`).
- **Card data.** Card numbers never reach LeadForge (Paystack's hosted page). Only the masked authorization (brand, last4, expiry month and year, bank, card type, channel) is kept on the payment. Stored webhook payloads have `authorization_code` (which could charge the card again with our key), the card signature and the BIN removed before they are written.
- **Credits.** Every balance change happens under `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` on the billing customer row with the ledger insert and the cached balance in one transaction. A refund or reversal can drive the balance negative; a negative balance refuses new holds (`CREDIT_BALANCE_NEGATIVE`), so refunded credits cannot keep being spent.
- **Permissions.** `billing:manage` (owner, admin): overview, plans, checkout, payments, verify, invoices, cancel, resume, card page. `billing:refund` (owner only, new in Phase 6): refunds. `GET /billing/banners` is open to every member and returns only the negative-balance banner to roles without `billing:manage`. Invoice downloads use HMAC links bound to organization, invoice and user (15 minutes), and re-check membership and `billing:manage` at download time. Another organization's payment, refund, invoice or link is `404` or `403`, never data (tested).
- **Resumable checkout (Stage 9).** The Paystack `authorization_url` returned by `POST /transaction/initialize` is stored on the payment row (it was already, for `Idempotency-Key` replays) and is shown again as `BillingOverview.pendingSubscription.authorizationUrl` so an owner or admin can resume an abandoned subscription checkout. Anyone holding that URL can only pay that checkout's server-set amount for this organization, but it is still treated as sensitive: only `billing:manage` reads the overview, responses are `Cache-Control: no-store`, and it is never logged. It is offered only while the payment is `pending`, not under review, Paystack is configured and the checkout started less than 30 minutes ago (`CHECKOUT_RESUME_WINDOW_MINUTES`), and never once the payment is final. Paystack's documentation states no lifetime for an authorization URL (checked 2026-10-27, see below), so the window is an assumption; paying through the URL still settles only after verification, as for any checkout.
- **Audit.** `billing_audit` (PostgreSQL) records every state change with before and after: checkout created, payment succeeded, failed, abandoned, verification mismatch, subscription transitions, linking, cancel and resume requests, refunds requested, processed and failed, disputes. Application audit events (`billing.*`) carry ids and statuses only.
- **Reconciliation** repairs lost webhooks and stuck states every 10 minutes and records each pass in `reconciliation_runs` (docs/DATABASE_DESIGN.md section 5).

## 13. Phase 7 security review (2026-10-27)

Stage 10 addendum (2026-10-28), new controls with their tests:

- **Re-authentication for a destructive action.** `POST /organizations/current/deletion-requests` needs the typed organization name and the owner's current password; wrong passwords count toward the per-account sign-in lock (10 per 15 minutes) and answer `403 REAUTH_FAILED` (a 401 would sign the browser out); the route is also limited to 10 per minute (`org-lifecycle.test.ts`).
- **Grace-period refusals** checked by `PermissionsGuard` after the permission (`@PausedDuringDeletionGrace`), so the permission matrix is unchanged (`permissions.guard.test.ts`, `org-lifecycle.test.ts`).
- **Organization data export**: owner only; HMAC links with the `org-export:` namespace bound to owner, organization and export; the download re-checks the owner role; failed browser downloads 303 to Settings (`org-lifecycle.test.ts`, `route-isolation.test.ts`, which also replays A's organization-export link as B and forges it).
- **Purge isolation**: the Mongo purge filters on the exact organization id, its collection list is derived from the schema registry with a unit test and a live-database coverage test; the purge test proves the other organization, a member of both organizations and the platform catalogue are byte-for-byte unchanged (`organization-scope.test.ts`, `org-purge.test.ts`).
- **Route and job inventory**: `route-isolation.test.ts` now classifies 159 routes (6 new) and 27 job types (3 new: `exports:build-organization-export` rejects another organization's export id; `maintenance:purge-organization` is a no-op for an organization that is not past its grace; `maintenance:scan-organization-deletions` is platform-level).

Every control in sections 1 to 12 was checked in code and tests against the release (backend: `apps/api`, `apps/worker`, `packages/*` except `ui`). Status: **Pass** (in place and tested), **Fixed** (a gap found and closed in Phase 7, with a test), **Partial** (in place with a documented limit), **Accepted** (a known risk kept on purpose, with the reason), **Pending** (needs an operator or the owner; listed in docs/DEPLOYMENT.md section 10).
