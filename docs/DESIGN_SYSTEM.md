## 1. Direction and principles

One token layer, one component library, three expressions:

| Surface        | Reference language                                        | Character                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Marketing site | google.com/chrome and its subpages; Material 3 Expressive | Supabase-sized type (48px maximum), tonal containers with 16 to 32px radii, pill CTAs, smooth scrolling with scrubbed, spring-smoothed motion, product mocks built from real components |
| Auth           | Google-account style flows                                | One centred card on a tonal plane, floating-label outlined fields, minimal chrome                                                                                                       |
| Dashboard      | Google Cloud console, Workspace, Analytics; Material 3    | Top app bar with search, navigation drawer or rail with a pill active indicator, tonal shell plane with a content panel, airy but dense tables, filter chips, snackbars                 |

Principles:

1. **Tone before shadow.** Layers are separated by surface-container tone. Shadows exist only for floating layers (menus, dialogs, snackbars).
2. **Pill controls.** Buttons, chips, segmented buttons and search bars are fully rounded.
3. **Motion you can see and feel.** Smooth and unhurried, scrubbed to scroll on marketing pages, real transitions on every state change everywhere, one easing family, always optional under reduced motion (section 8).
4. **Honest data.** Status is never colour alone, missing data says so, sample data is labelled "(Sample)" (section 12).
5. **LeadForge identity only.** We adopt Google's design _language_ (type, shape, tonal surfaces, motion, density). We never use Google's logos, product names as branding, the four-colour Google brand palette as our identity, screenshots or any proprietary asset. LeadForge keeps its own name, mark and brand colour.

## 2. Colour

### 2.1 Brand: Forge Cobalt

Seed hue 279 (HCT), chroma 72. Tonal palettes for primary, secondary (hue 272, chroma 24), tertiary (hue 190, chroma 40), neutral (hue 262, chroma 5), neutral variant (hue 262, chroma 10) and the four status hues (success 150, warning 75, error 25, info 240) were generated with Material Color Utilities (HCT tonal palettes) and mapped to roles with the standard Material 3 tone assignments.

Why cobalt instead of the Phase 1 green:

- **Semantics.** Verified, completed and success states appear on nearly every data screen. With a green brand, every primary button competed with "verified". A cobalt primary leaves green exclusively to success.
- **Trust.** A confident blue reads as enterprise and data-centric, which suits a lead-intelligence product.
- **Distinctness.** Hue 279 sits clearly on the violet side of Google Blue (HCT hue about 266 to 270), so the brand is not a Google look-alike.

### 2.3 Contrast rules

- Text and its background: at least 4.5:1. UI boundaries that identify a control or its state, and focus indicators: at least 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).
- `--border` is **decorative** (card outlines, dividers) and is not a control boundary. Anything a user must perceive to operate a control (field outline, checkbox box, outlined button, switch track) uses `--input`, which passes 3:1 on every surface.
- Verified by script, not by eye: `pnpm --filter @leadforge/ui check:contrast` (also runs as part of the package `test` script). It parses `tokens.css`, checks that both dark blocks match, and tests every text pair (foreground, muted, brand, status, inverse on every surface they can sit on) and every UI pair (input and ring on every surface). Current result: **220 pairs checked across light and dark, 0 failures**. Tightest pairs: light `info` on `surface-container-high` 5.27:1; dark `inverse-primary` on `inverse-surface` 4.99:1.
- Any new token or new pairing must be added to `PAIRS` in `packages/ui/scripts/check-contrast.mjs`.

## 3. Typography

### 3.1 Families

- **Google Sans Flex** (SIL Open Font License 1.1). Published on Google Fonts in 2025 alongside the open-sourcing of Google Sans; multi-axis variable font. The installed Next 15.5 does not list it in `next/font/google`'s font data (it first appears in Next 16), so it is self-hosted through `next/font/local` from `@fontsource-variable/google-sans-flex`, using the latin file that carries the weight (1 to 1000) and optical-size axes (117 KB, `google-sans-flex-latin-opsz-normal.woff2`). `font-optical-sizing: auto` gives display sizes their tighter optical forms. CSS variable `--font-brand-sans`, `display: swap`, Arial-adjusted fallback.
- **Google Sans Code** (SIL OFL 1.1) as the matching monospace for IDs, domains and code: `next/font/local` from `@fontsource-variable/google-sans-code` (latin wght file, 300 to 800, 35 KB). Variable `--font-brand-mono`.
- No runtime CDN: both files are bundled by next/font. Declared in `apps/web/app/fonts.ts`.
- Fallback stacks: `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, "Segoe UI", Roboto, "Helvetica Neue", sans-serif` and `ui-monospace, "Cascadia Mono", Consolas, "Liberation Mono", monospace`. Characters outside the latin subset (for example Yoruba diacritics such as ẹ and ṣ, or ₦) render in the fallback face.

## 5. Elevation

- Default: tone. A card is `surface` with a decorative outline, or a `surface-container-*` fill. Hover is a state layer, not a shadow.
- `--shadow-1`: elevated card variant and resting floating elements.
- `--shadow-2`: menus, select lists, popovers, combobox lists, chart tooltips.
- `--shadow-3`: dialogs (from 480px), snackbars.
- Dark mode shadows are stronger (`rgb(0 0 0 / 0.4)` and up) but tone still does most of the work (`surface-elevated` is `surface-container-high`).

## 7. Responsive rules (240px baseline)

Breakpoints are mobile-first; base styles target 240px.

| Name                | Min width | Notes                                                                                              |
| ------------------- | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| base                | 240px     | single column, 12px gutters, stacked controls                                                      |
| `xs`                | 360px     | 16px gutters, inline button pairs allowed                                                          |
| 480 (`min-[480px]`) | 480px     | dialogs stop being full-screen; table rows stop becoming cards; bulk bars and pagination go inline |
| `sm`                | 640px     | two-column cards, horizontal stepper                                                               |
| `md`                | 768px     | tablet; inline filters; marketing nav links                                                        |
| `lg`                | 1024px    | fixed navigation drawer; pinned scrollytelling                                                     |
| `xl`                | 1280px    | 32px gutters                                                                                       |
| `2xl`               | 1440px    | content max width reached                                                                          |

`useMediaQuery(MEDIA.narrow | MEDIA.belowMd | MEDIA.lg)` exposes the same breakpoints to components that must switch structure rather than style.

Hard rules:

1. `html, body { overflow-x: clip }` is a safety net, not a strategy. No layout may depend on it. CI fails if `document.documentElement.scrollWidth > window.innerWidth` at any tested width, including after scrolling through marketing pages and to the bottom of shell pages.
2. No fixed widths above 216px outside scroll containers. Flex and grid children get `min-width: 0`; media gets `max-width: 100%`.
3. Tables live inside `ScrollRegion`: an `overflow-x: auto` container with edge fades that appear only when content continues past that edge, and a keyboard-focusable region (`tabIndex=0`, `role="region"`, labelled). Bulk-action bars and pagination stack vertically under 480px.
4. Dialogs and Sheets are full-screen under 480px with sticky header and footer.
5. Inputs and buttons are full width in forms under 360px. Button groups stack.
6. Topbar at 240px holds a 40px menu button, a truncated title and one overflow menu. Nothing else.
7. Long tokens (domains, emails, IDs) use `overflow-wrap: anywhere` plus a copy button (`CopyableValue`), never truncation that hides the only copy of the value.
8. Charts switch to a compact variant (no axis labels, shorter plot) with the Chart/Table switch under 480px.
9. Typography uses `clamp()` with minimums safe for a 216px content width.
10. Test matrix: 240, 320, 375, 768, 1024, 1440px. 240px at 100 percent zoom is the floor.

## 8. Motion

Direction (user feedback, 2026-10-25): motion must be clearly visible, smooth and unhurried, the way google.com/chrome feels. No instant pop-ins and no instant colour swaps. This supersedes the Phase 0 "300ms once per session" budget and the earlier "no smooth scrolling" rule.

### 8.1 Tokens

| Token                                                         | Value                                                                            | Use                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `--ease-emphasized` / `EASE_EMPHASIZED`                       | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`                                                     | default for anything that moves or changes tone      |
| `--ease-emphasized-decelerate` / `EASE_EMPHASIZED_DECELERATE` | `cubic-bezier(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1)`                                                | entering: reveals, page transitions, dialogs, sheets |
| `--ease-emphasized-accelerate` / `EASE_EMPHASIZED_ACCELERATE` | `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.8, 0.15)`                                                | leaving: sheet close, scrollytelling exit            |
| `--ease-standard`                                             | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`                                                     | state layers                                         |
| `DURATION`                                                    | short 200ms, medium 300ms, long 500ms, extraLong 700ms, reveal 900ms, page 400ms |                                                      |
| `STAGGER`                                                     | 100ms                                                                            | sibling delay in reveal groups (80 to 120ms allowed) |
| `SCROLL_SPRING`                                               | stiffness 140, damping 30, mass 0.6                                              | `useSpring` smoothing of every scroll-linked value   |
| `SPRING.fast` / `.default` / `.slow`                          | stiffness/damping 1400/67, 700/48, 300/31 (mass 1)                               | M3 Expressive spatial springs; indicators use `slow` |

### 8.2 Rules

1. Every non-essential animation goes through `useMotionSafe()`, which is false during server rendering and under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. Components then render their **final state**: no scroll-linked transforms, no smooth scrolling, no autoplay. `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"` wraps the app as a second guard, and the base layer clamps CSS animation and transition durations under reduced motion.
2. **Smooth scrolling on marketing pages only**: Lenis (MIT), `lerp: 0.09`, driven from framer-motion's frame loop (`frame.update`) so scroll-linked values read the same smoothed position every frame (`apps/web/components/marketing/smooth-scroll.tsx`). The native scrollbar stays; keyboard, focus and other native scrolling keep working (Lenis resyncs on native scroll events); in-page anchors glide with a 128px offset for the sticky chrome; nested scroll containers (carousel track, chip bars), dialogs, menus, listboxes and popovers keep native scrolling (`allowNestedScroll`, `prevent`), and the page never moves while Radix holds a scroll lock. Not mounted under reduced motion. The dashboard and auth pages keep native scroll, because Lenis fights nested scroll containers and tables.
3. Pinned sections are scrubbed by scroll position, never scroll-locked.
4. No autoplay without a visible pause control.
5. Prefer `transform` and `opacity` for movement. Colour, background, border and shadow changes on interactive elements transition (250ms, emphasized) through the `transition-ui` utility; nothing swaps instantly.
6. Hero text is server-rendered, visible without JavaScript at full opacity, and never remounted after hydration (motion styles attach to the same element); it only moves once the user scrolls, so LCP never waits on motion. Page transitions skip the first load.
7. No animation on polling updates inside tables. A table body crossfades once when it replaces its skeleton, and mobile cards present on the first data render stagger in once; rows that arrive later appear in place.
8. Never animate numbers that are not real backend data.
9. Elements with a scroll-linked transform carry `data-motion="scroll-linked"`; e2e asserts they are untransformed under reduced motion.

### 8.3 Transitions everywhere

| Pattern                                | Spec                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Route transitions (`PageTransition`)   | `template.tsx` in `(marketing)` and `(app)/app`: fade plus 16px rise, 400ms emphasized decelerate on client navigations; the first load renders immediately |
| `transition-ui` utility                | colour, background, border, outline, shadow, opacity and transform over 250ms emphasized: buttons, chips, segments, tabs, fields, nav items, cards          |
| State layer (`state-layer`)            | `currentColor` overlay at 8% hover, 10% focus-visible, 12% pressed; 250ms opacity                                                                           |
| Press (`press-scale`)                  | `scale: 0.97` on `:active` (the `scale` property, so it composes with translate utilities); none under reduced motion                                       |
| Card hover (`Card interactive`)        | 2px lift plus state layer, 300ms                                                                                                                            |
| Menus, selects, popovers, tooltips     | fade plus zoom from 95%, 200ms                                                                                                                              |

## 9. Charts

- **Library: Recharts 3.** SVG rendered by React (crisp, inspectable, themable), colours from CSS variables so light and dark come from tokens, `ResponsiveContainer` for fluid width, a built-in keyboard accessibility layer, and broad adoption. ### 10.2 DataTable

Server-side table on TanStack Table (`manualSorting`, `manualPagination`, `manualFiltering`).

- Required: `columns`, `data`, `getRowId` (stable IDs keep selection across pages), `caption` (names both the table and its scroll region).
- Sorting: pass `sorting` and `onSortingChange`; headers become buttons and `th` gets `aria-sort`. Without `onSortingChange` headers are plain text.
- Selection: `enableRowSelection` (boolean or per-row function), `rowSelection`, `onRowSelectionChange`, `bulkActions(context)` where `context` has `ids` (all selected IDs, across pages), `rows` (selected rows on this page) and `clear()`. The bulk-action bar (`role="region"`, "Bulk actions") shows the count and stacks under 480px, where it also sticks 64px from the top (below the 56px app bar) so it stays reachable while cards further down are selected.
- View: `columnVisibility` and `onColumnVisibilityChange` with a "Show or hide columns" menu (`enableColumnVisibility`), and a "Row density" menu (`density`, `onDensityChange`, `enableDensityToggle`): comfortable 52px rows at 13px, compact 40px rows at 12px.
- Layout: `stickyFirstColumn` pins the selection column and the first data column; `mobileCards` (true or `{ render }`) renders each row as a stacked card under 480px, using the column with `meta.mobileTitle` (or the first data column) as the title and the rest as a label/value list. The column menu is hidden while rows are cards.
- Widths: once a table is wider than its scroll region, table layout ignores widths on `<td>` and shrinks columns to their min-content width (and `overflow-wrap: anywhere` makes that one character). Size cell _content_ instead: wrap it in a block with a width from 480px (the app's `CellBox`), use `break-words`, and let the region scroll sideways. The selection column holds a fixed 32px wrapper so it is exactly 48px, which is what `stickyFirstColumn` offsets the first data column by.
- Slots: `toolbar` (search, FilterBar), `footer` (Pagination), `loading` with `skeletonRows`, `empty` (EmptyState), `error` (ErrorState).
- Column `meta` (module augmentation of TanStack's `ColumnMeta`): `label` (plain-text name for menus and mobile cards when `header` is not a string), `align` (`start`, `center`, `end`; `end` adds `tabular-nums`), `hideOnMobile`, `mobileTitle`, `mobileActions` (row actions rendered unlabelled at the end of the mobile card), `headerClassName`, `cellClassName`.
- No per-row animation on data updates. The body crossfades once when it replaces the skeleton; hover and selection tones transition.

### 10.3 Usage patterns later phases must follow

1. **Lists**: `PageHeader` + `FilterBar` (with a `SearchField` in `search`) passed as `DataTable` `toolbar`, `Pagination` as `footer`, server-side sorting and cursor pagination wired to the query. Loading, empty (`EmptyState` that explains and offers the first action) and error (`ErrorState` with retry and request ID) are always implemented.
2. **Field and job state**: every contact field state uses `FieldStatus`; every job state uses `JobStatus`. Never ad-hoc badges for these.
3. **Metrics**: `Stat` with `value={null}` until real data exists; trends only from backend comparisons; `footnote` names the source and freshness.
4. **Identifiers**: IDs, domains, emails and URLs that users copy use `CopyableValue` (or `Code` when copying is not useful). Never truncate the only copy.
5. **Account conditions** (failed payment, credits low, source disabled): `Banner` at the top of the content area; destructive banners are not dismissable until resolved.
6. **Destructive bulk actions**: `ConfirmDialog` with `destructive`, a `confirmPhrase` that includes the count (for example `SUPPRESS 24`) and the consequences in `children`.
7. **Provenance and activity**: `Timeline`, each entry with source or actor in `meta`.
8. **Discovery wizard**: `Stepper` with `onStepSelect` for completed steps, one primary action per step (section 10.5).
9. **Feedback**: `toast` for the result of a user action (saved, sent, revoked); inline `ErrorState` for failed loads; never a toast for page-load failures.
10. **Unavailable features**: disabled control with a visible "Coming soon" label and no route.

## 11. Accessibility

Target: WCAG 2.1 AA on every surface (ARCHITECTURE section 10 requires it for dashboard controls; marketing, legal and auth pages meet it too), keyboard-only use of every dashboard page (PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS section 10), and working screen-reader output.

### 11.1 Rules

- Interactive components come from Radix or native elements. No `div` buttons.
- Focus is always visible: 2px `--ring` with 2px offset on `:focus-visible`; fields use their 2px primary outline; tabs use an inset ring; Radix tab panels (which are Tab stops) show the standard ring. `forced-colors` mode maps the ring to `Highlight`. Never remove an outline without a replacement; `outline-none` is only for elements that take programmatic focus (a wizard step heading).
- Skip-to-content link on every layout, targeting `#main`; every layout (marketing, legal, auth, dashboard, not-found, error) renders exactly one `main#main`.
- Headings: one `h1` per page (`PageHeader`, the marketing hero, or `ErrorState titleAs="h1"` when an error is the whole page), and levels never skip. Levels come from the `HeadingLevel` context, not from the tag you type: pages start at 2; `Card`, `DialogContent` and `SheetContent` go one deeper; `CardTitle`, `EmptyState` and `Heading` render the current level. A page section with its own heading renders it with `<Heading>` and wraps its content in `<HeadingLevel>`. A card without a title that holds an `EmptyState` passes `level={useHeadingLevel()}`. Visual size comes from classes, never from the tag.
- Landmarks: a banner (top app bar or site nav), named `nav`s ("Dashboard", "Primary", "Breadcrumb", "Pagination", "Import steps"), one `main` and labelled `section`s. Search landmarks are named (`SearchField` uses its `label`; the top-bar search is "Pages and settings"). A `DataTable` scroll region is named "_caption_ table", so it never duplicates the section that holds it.
- Names: every icon button has an `aria-label`. When a control shows text, its accessible name starts with that text (WCAG 2.5.3, label in name): the credits chip reads "877 credits available…", the account button "AO, Account menu for …", field-style filter triggers are named label plus value ("Region Lagos"), and filter chips are named by their visible text and describe the full applied list ("Applied: Lagos, Abuja, Kano").
- Links inside running text (a sentence, a definition, a caption) use the `link-inline` utility: always underlined (WCAG 1.4.1). Standalone links (table cells, card titles, navigation) may underline on hover only.
- Status is never colour alone: `StatusIndicator` (marked `data-status-tone`) always renders an icon and text; trends carry a signed value; chart series have a legend and a table view.
- Live regions: toasts (polite), copy confirmations, bulk-selection count and pagination summary. Status transitions use `LiveAnnouncement` (polite, atomic, visually hidden): the discovery job and website analysis pages, the import wizard, the business Website analysis tab, contact collection and email checks, and the score recompute. The message is derived from the status, so it changes on transitions only, never on every poll. Destructive banners and form errors use `role="alert"`.
- Loading placeholders are `role="status"` with `aria-busy="true"` and a name ("Loading businesses").
- Form errors are linked by `aria-describedby` and set `aria-invalid`. A group error (the scoring weight total, a fieldset rule) is linked from every field or the fieldset it concerns. Wizards focus the first invalid field in display order.
- Focus management: dialogs and sheets trap focus, close on Escape and return focus to whatever opened them (`useReturnFocus` inside `DialogContent` and `SheetContent`, which also covers dialogs opened from state rather than a `DialogTrigger`). Menus follow the WAI-ARIA menu button pattern and are non-modal (`DropdownMenu` defaults to `modal={false}`), so an open menu never hides the page from assistive technology. Wizards move focus to the new step's heading after a step change (discovery and import). Popup lists navigated with `aria-activedescendant` are `tabIndex={-1}` so they never become Tab stops.
- Scroll containers that hold content (tables, chart tables, the marketing carousel track) are focusable and labelled.
- Carousels have a visible pause control and slide labels; nothing auto-advances under reduced motion.
- Reduced motion renders final states: no Lenis, no scroll-linked transforms, no reveal left at partial opacity.
- Keyboard-only and screen-reader passes on the directory, job wizard, billing checkout and pipeline before each release (section 11.4).

### 11.4 Manual screen-reader checklist

Automated checks cannot judge what a screen reader actually says. Before each release a person runs these with NVDA (current release) on Firefox and on Chrome (Windows), and VoiceOver on Safari (macOS and iOS), and records the date, versions and findings in the release notes.

1. Landmarks and headings: the landmarks list (NVDA `D`, the VoiceOver rotor) shows the banner, the "Dashboard" navigation, main and named regions; the headings list starts with one level-1 heading and reads as an outline of the page.
2. Skip link: the first Tab announces "Skip to content"; activating it moves the reading position into the main content.
3. Sign in: labels, "required" and the errors are announced when the form is submitted empty; after signing in the new page is announced.
4. Shell: the navigation drawer announces as a dialog named "Navigation"; the current page is announced as current; the org switcher and account menu announce as menus with their items; the bell announces the unread count, and each unread item starts with "Unread".
5. Directory: the table is announced with its caption, column headers and sort state; selecting a row announces "1 selected"; the pagination summary is announced after Next; filter chips announce their applied values.
6. Business detail: tabs announce their position and the disabled "Soon" tabs; the corrections dialog announces its title and description; errors are read with their field.
7. CSV import: the file button, upload progress, the mapping selects ("Import “Business Name” as") and each step heading are announced as focus moves; the status change is announced once ("Import Completed with failed rows"), not on every poll.
8. Discovery wizard: each step heading is announced when focus moves to it; validation errors are read with the invalid field; the estimate, blockers and the insufficient-credits message are read in order; on the job page, status changes are announced.
9. Website analysis and contact collection: queued, running and finished are each announced once; evidence snippets are read as text.
10. Duplicate merge: the radio groups announce their names ("Surviving record", "Name to keep"); the result toast is announced.
11. Scoring settings: each weight's description and the total error are read with the field; the recompute status change is announced.
12. Toasts are announced without moving focus; destructive confirmations announce as alert dialogs, with focus on Cancel (or the typed-phrase field).
13. Browser zoom at 200% and 400%, and Windows High Contrast (forced colours): content reflows, focus rings stay visible, and status icons keep their text.
14. With the operating system's reduce-motion setting on, marketing pages scroll natively and nothing animates on scroll.
15. Pipeline: each card is announced by its business heading; `]` and `[` move the focused card, focus stays on the card in its new column, and the move is announced once ("Moved … to Contacted"). The log-activity dialog announces the suppression step and its reason field; bulk and export job pages announce status changes once.
16. Billing: the plans' disabled buy buttons are read with their reason; the Paystack return page announces “Confirming the payment” and then its final state once (never success before the server confirms); refund and cancel confirmations announce as alert dialogs with focus on Cancel, and the refund consequences are read before the fields. Settings → Pipeline stages: Move up and Move down announce the new position and keep focus on the button; a version conflict moves focus to the explanation. Lead details and the next attempt announce as dialogs with their fields' hints.
